How to win on Devpost

Summarised from 33 Shipaton livestreams (2025 and 2026): kickoff, "how to win with the judges", the final AMA, OneSignal, paywall, pricing, app-approval and ASO sessions.
The 5 things that matter most
  1. The video decides. Pre-screeners watch only the first 2 minutes. Show the real app and a one-sentence pitch within 10 seconds.
  2. Category answers come next. Short, in your own words, one concrete detail and one real number each.
  3. Never show what isn't in the app. Finalists' apps are downloaded and compared with the video. A mismatch means disqualification.
  4. Be live and reachable. Working store link, available in the US with in-app purchases, video public or unlisted (a private YouTube video fails the automatic check).
  5. An app can win only one category. Entering several is free, but focus your effort on where you can actually win; "four is already a lot".

How judging works

  1. Automatic filter (right after the deadline): release date inside the window, working store link, US availability including IAPs, required fields filled, video on an allowed host. Nobody is told if they were filtered out.
  2. Pre-screen: RevenueCat staff each take ~20 apps, watch only the first 2 minutes, read the category answers and description, and score each category you entered 1–5.
  3. Judges score the top apps; sponsors pick their own category winners.
  4. Finalists (~top 5 per category): the app is downloaded and checked against the video.

Priority: video > category answers > description (long ones get skimmed) > the app itself. The version judged is what's live in October.

The video

DO
  • Real app on screen + pitch in the first 10 seconds.
  • Who it's for → the problem → the solution → the "aha" moment.
  • Name the categories you're entering and give each a short beat.
  • Zoom in on 1–2 details and explain the decision (great for Design).
  • Show growth numbers for 5–10 seconds.
  • AI voice-over and AI B-roll are fine if they don't misrepresent the app.
DON'T
  • A cinematic AI ad that barely shows the app.
  • Mocked or animated UI that isn't in the live build.
  • Features that only exist on a platform that isn't live yet.
  • Fully AI-written answers; judges notice the repeated phrasing.
  • Long descriptions.

Paywall & pricing tips (expert sessions)

  • 1–2 plans, default to Annual with "view all plans"; free trial on Annual only, and make it unmissable ("Start my 7-day free trial").
  • Lifetime is an anchor and should feel "shockingly expensive" (about 2–3× annual).
  • Lead with value; real one-line reviews beat feature lists.
  • LTV is the north-star metric. With little traffic, skip A/B tests and make bold changes; fix the top of the funnel first.
  • Hard paywalls convert ~5× more, but freemium spreads by word of mouth.

Store, review & ASO

  • The app must be public in the US by the deadline; "in review" doesn't count as live.
  • Apple scrutinises health/medical claims: avoid "improves adherence", "doctor-recommended" in listing and video.
  • Screenshots must match the real UI; no "coming soon"; the iOS listing must not mention ads or Android; include a Terms of Use URL.
  • Small, focused updates get reviewed faster.
  • ASO: top 1–2 keywords in the title, the next tier in the subtitle, never repeat words; target long-tail problem phrases; the first screenshot's headline should read in 1 second; promotional text can change without review; nominate the app for featuring.
  • Winners also get a Times Square demo day.

Final checklist before you submit

  1. Store link works and the app is available in the US (with IAPs).
  2. Video public or unlisted, real app in the first 10 seconds, under the time limit.
  3. Every category answer filled: short, specific, your own words, one real number.
  4. Numbers match everywhere (story, answers, video).
  5. Promo codes or trial instructions for judges.
  6. Ask a friend to watch the first 2 minutes against the rules.
